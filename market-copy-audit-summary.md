## Overview

## Summary of Substantive Changes

### 1. Africa Voluntary Carbon Market Figures
* **Previous framing:** Stated that Africa's voluntary carbon market is projected to grow from **\$15 billion today to \$122 billion by 2033**.
* **Why it needed fixing:** Even the entire *global* voluntary carbon market currently transacts roughly \$0.5B–\$2.8B annually (with Africa accounting for ~10–16% of issuances), so "\$15B today" is inaccurate for current market size. In addition, the **\$120B+** benchmark comes from the Africa Carbon Markets Initiative (ACMI) **2050** roadmap—not 2033.
* **Updated framing:** Now cites the verified **ACMI targets** of **\$6 billion in annual revenue by 2030** and **\$120 billion+ by 2050**, alongside **\$22 billion** in global carbon-project and offtake capital commitments recorded in 2025.

---

### 2. EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) Timing & Scope
* **Previous framing:** One section stated the EUDR *"now restricts"* imports, while others cited December 2026, and multiple pages described bamboo as *"EUDR-compliant."*
* **Why it needed fixing:**
  1. **Enforcement date:** Under EU Regulation 2025/2650, EUDR obligations take effect on **30 December 2026** for large and medium operators (and **30 June 2027** for micro and small enterprises), so the site needed a consistent timeline.
  2. **Legal status of bamboo:** Under EU Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/2102, **100% bamboo products are explicitly excluded** from the EUDR's regulated wood scope because bamboo is botanically a woody grass, not timber.
* **Updated framing:** Unified all references around the **30 December 2026 / June 2027** enforcement dates and updated the positioning to highlight that **pure bamboo products are exempt from EUDR wood due-diligence filing obligations**—an even stronger commercial advantage for European buyers—while noting that Bamboostic still maintains land-use and supply-chain records to support buyer ESG due diligence.

---

### 3. Nigeria's Forest Cover & Global Tree Loss
* **Previous framing:** Stated that Nigeria's forest cover fell from **10% in 1996 to 6% today**, and that **15 billion trees are harvested** globally each year.
* **Why it needed fixing:**
  * Nigeria originally designated ~10% of its land area as forest reserves earlier in the 20th century, and effective productive forest cover had *already* fallen below 6% by the late 1990s (1996–1998 FORMECU data)—not 10% in 1996. Distinguishing effective natural forest cover from broader FAO definitions (which include plantations and secondary woodlands) avoids confusion.
  * Globally, the ~15 billion annual figure covers *total tree loss* (including wildfires and land clearing), not solely commercial harvesting.
* **Updated framing:** Clarified that Nigeria originally gazetted ~10% as forest reserves while effective productive forest cover fell below 6% by the late 1990s with primary natural forests severely depleted today, and adjusted the global figure to **~15 billion trees lost globally each year** (vs. ~5 billion regrowing/planted).

---

### 4. Nigeria Housing Deficit & Construction Industry Size
* **Previous framing:** One section described Nigeria's 20M+ housing deficit as *"a ₦6.5 trillion construction opportunity,"* conflating the housing gap with the construction sector's annual GDP output.
* **Why it needed fixing:** **₦6.5 trillion** represents the Nigerian construction sector's total nominal GDP contribution in **2024**, not the cost or market value of closing a 20M+ unit housing deficit.
* **Updated framing:** Clarified across all sections that Nigeria's **20M+ unit housing deficit** underpins a construction industry that contributed **~₦6.5 trillion to GDP in 2024** (and continues expanding in nominal terms).

## Claims Verified as Accurate (No Changes Needed)
* **Global bamboo market:** ~\$79B (2024) projected to reach \$100–130B by 2033 at ~6% CAGR.
* **Harvest cycle:** 3–5 years to structural maturity with regeneration from established root systems and no annual replanting.
* **Strand-woven bamboo specs:** 3,200+ lbf Janka hardness and 1,100–1,250 kg/m³ density (more than twice the hardness of oak).
